Materials science · Quantum physics · Computation

Hi, I’m Edward Deleu.

I’m a PhD student in Materials Science and Engineering at Stanford, working with Prof. Felipe Jornada as an NSF Graduate Research Fellow. I use computation to understand how materials behave at the quantum scale.

Currently, I’m studying excited-state reaction pathways of four-carbon defects in monolayer hexagonal boron nitride (hBN), focusing on interconversion between single-photon emitters (more about this work). Previously, I studied Electrical and Computer Engineering at Princeton.

Outside research, I enjoy hiking, cycling, reading, cooking, baking, and a good cup of tea. More about me →

Selected projects

PhD researchStanford MSE · Prof. Felipe Jornada · 2026–present

Excited-state pathways of carbon defects in hBN

Can one carbon–nitrogen exchange turn a candidate yellow single-photon emitter in hBN into a candidate blue one? First-principles reaction pathways, from DFT to GW–BSE excited-state forces.

Research summary

Senior thesisPrinceton ECE · Prof. Saien Xie · 2025–26

Strain engineering of transition metal dichalcogenides

A combined first-principles and experimental study of how mechanical strain tunes the band structure of monolayer TMD semiconductors.
